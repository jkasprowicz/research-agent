# Reviewer 1

## Overall assessment

Recommendation: `Minor Revision`

This manuscript presents an exploratory analysis of routine laboratory data to identify recurrent phenotypic profiles in real-world clinical encounters. For the scope of Revista Interfaces, the topic is relevant, interdisciplinary, and reasonably well written. The article has improved substantially in coherence, restraint, and practical framing.

## Strengths

- The study addresses a real and relevant problem: how to interpret heterogeneous laboratory patterns beyond isolated markers.
- The manuscript now presents itself appropriately as exploratory laboratory phenotyping rather than as a mature predictive or diagnostic system.
- The discussion is cautious and clinically grounded.
- The robustness section improves credibility by clarifying the role of age, repeated encounters, and testing-intensity selection.
- The article fits the journal’s interdisciplinary health-and-technology profile.

## Weaknesses

- The manuscript still depends heavily on an encounter-level interpretation, which limits direct patient-level conclusions.
- The cluster separation is modest, and the article appropriately acknowledges that, but some readers may still see the phenotypes as only partially resolved.
- The care-setting analysis is weak in effect size and should be understood as descriptive only.
- The clinical relevance is plausible, but still more conceptual than operational.

## Reviewer judgment

From a scientific standpoint, this is acceptable for a journal that values applied exploratory work using real-world health data. I do not see a need for methodological redesign under the frozen-scope assumption. My recommendation is revision mainly for presentation clarity and final editorial formatting, not for substantial scientific rework.

## Specific recommendation

`Minor Revision`

Reason:

- scientific content is adequate for scope
- claims are mostly bounded correctly
- remaining concerns are mainly editorial and presentational
