# Reviewer 2

## Overall assessment

Recommendation: `Major Revision`

The manuscript is interesting and potentially publishable, but it remains scientifically modest. Its main contribution is descriptive: identifying three laboratory-based profiles in a selected multimarker-rich cohort. The article is strongest when interpreted as exploratory health informatics. It is less convincing if interpreted as demonstrating clinically meaningful subtypes.

## Strengths

- The manuscript uses real-world laboratory data from a large set of clinical encounters.
- The authors avoid the most common overclaims of machine-learning manuscripts.
- The discussion now acknowledges age structure, repeated encounters, and selection by laboratory density.
- The paper is readable and much more mature than a purely technical clustering report.

## Weaknesses

- The cohort is clearly selected by testing density, which limits general interpretation.
- The age structure appears influential, and although the no-age sensitivity analysis helps, it does not eliminate concern that age remains a major organizing axis.
- Repeated encounters change prevalence estimates, which weakens confidence in the apparent population composition of the phenotypes.
- The paper is better at describing clusters than at proving their broader clinical importance.
- The contribution may feel incremental to reviewers expecting stronger translational payoff.

## Reviewer judgment

I would not reject the paper outright for this journal, but I would hesitate to accept it without careful editorial framing. The paper should be read as an exploratory descriptive contribution in applied health analytics, not as a validated clinical phenotyping framework.

## Specific recommendation

`Major Revision`

Reason:

- not because the science is unsound
- but because the practical meaning of the clusters remains limited
- and the editorial presentation must be flawless for this journal context
