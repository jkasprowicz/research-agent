# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Map recent AI applications for individualized emergency-department waiting-time prediction and identify practical issues for hospital decision makers.
- healthcare setting: Emergency departments and hospital operational environments represented in the reviewed studies.
- patient population: Not a primary patient cohort; review of studies using ED patients/encounters from electronic health records and operational systems.
- sample size: 8,613 citations identified; 15 studies included after screening and appraisal.
- study design: Scoping/systematized review with descriptive thematic analysis.

# Data
- data source: Bibliographic databases: CINAHL, EMBASE, MEDLINE, PsycINFO, Web of Science and PubMed.
- variables used: Study-level characteristics; ED queue features; staff/resource features; patient features; time-based features; model types and performance approaches.
- outcomes measured: Individualized waiting-time prediction performance and operational interpretability considerations.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Descriptive synthesis of included studies; no primary inferential model.
- machine learning methods: Reviewed Random Forest, linear regression, LASSO and other AI/ML models; emphasizes nonlinear models and queue-based features.
- simulation methods: Not a simulation study.
- queueing methods: Queue-based feature categories are discussed as predictors, but the review is mainly ML-oriented rather than analytical queueing.
- optimization methods: No direct optimization model; operational implications for wait-time communication and ED management.

# Results
- main findings: AI/ML approaches generally outperformed rolling-average waiting-time estimates used in hospitals. Queue-based and nonlinear modeling strategies appeared particularly useful.
- quantitative outcomes: 15 included studies from 8,613 initial citations; all AI/ML approaches reviewed outperformed traditional rolling-average baselines according to the authors.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: The literature is sparse and heterogeneous; most included studies are observational/proof-of-concept and use historical records.
- implicit limitations: Evidence is ED-specific and not directly about elective SUS waiting lists; implementation, calibration drift, fairness and workflow adoption remain underdeveloped.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 4
- justification: Highly relevant methodologically for waiting-time prediction, feature engineering and operational model design, but the setting is emergency care rather than SUS regulation/waiting lists.
