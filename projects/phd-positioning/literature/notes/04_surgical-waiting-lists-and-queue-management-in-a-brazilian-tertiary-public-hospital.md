# Citation
- title: Surgical waiting lists and queue management in a Brazilian tertiary public hospital
- authors: Antonio Pazin-Filho et al.
- year: 2024
- country: Brazil
- journal: BMC Health Services Research
- source file: /Users/joaokasprowicz/Documents/Projects/research-agent/projects/phd-positioning/literature/pdfs/tier1/12913_2024_Article_10735.pdf

# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Describe implementation of a centralized surgical-indication queue system and evaluate impact on waiting time, backlog and COVID-19 rationing.
- healthcare setting: Brazilian public tertiary university hospital.
- patient population: Patients with elective surgical indications registered in the Patients with Surgical Indication system.
- sample size: 82,844 surgical indications from 2016 to 2022.
- study design: Retrospective observational study of a queue-management implementation.

# Data
- data source: Hospital surgical indication system containing inflows, outflows, reasons for outflow, queue size and waiting time.
- variables used: Indications, backlog, waiting time, inflow/outflow counts, reason for outflow, oncological/non-oncological categories, pandemic periods.
- outcomes measured: Median waiting time, backlog size, monthly surgical output and rationing patterns.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Descriptive analysis and interrupted time-series/Newey-West regression for pandemic phases.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No simulation component.
- queueing methods: Uses queue-management principles and references Little’s Law; empirical queue management rather than predictive modeling.
- optimization methods: Centralized list management and rationing/prioritization processes.

# Results
- main findings: Centralizing surgical indications made queue management feasible, reduced median waiting time and backlog, and supported rationing during the pandemic.
- quantitative outcomes: Median waiting time decreased from 98 days in 2016 to 14 days in 2022; backlog decreased from 6,884 to 844; 82,844 indications were included.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Single-hospital retrospective implementation; institutional processes may limit generalization.
- implicit limitations: Very strong operational evidence but not a predictive or explainable computational model; causal attribution may be affected by broader institutional changes.
