# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Compare pre- and post-implementation periods of a cardiac-surgery access-management program for SUS users.
- healthcare setting: University hospital in Campinas, Brazil.
- patient population: SUS users undergoing elective cardiac surgery.
- sample size: 865 elective cardiac surgeries from 2013 to 2019.
- study design: Retrospective longitudinal observational pre-post study.

# Data
- data source: Medical records and hospital infection-control records.
- variables used: Waiting time in surgical queue, hospital stay, infection rate, demographic and clinical variables, procedure type.
- outcomes measured: Surgical waiting time, length of stay, number of surgeries and infection outcomes.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Descriptive statistics, Shapiro-Wilk normality test and pre-post statistical comparisons.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No simulation component.
- queueing methods: No formal queueing model; empirical access-management intervention.
- optimization methods: Bed-management and patient-access management program.

# Results
- main findings: The program significantly reduced waiting time for cardiac surgery and suggested improved throughput.
- quantitative outcomes: Median/central waiting time reduced from 97.50 days (95%CI 79.00-120.30) to 52.50 days (95%CI 44.40-63.20).

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Secondary data; inability to control severity/preoperative risk; potential selection bias among long-waiting patients.
- implicit limitations: Evaluates a management program but does not predict queue behavior or support prospective prioritization.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 5
- justification: Direct SUS surgical waiting-list evidence and useful benchmark for measurable operational outcomes.
