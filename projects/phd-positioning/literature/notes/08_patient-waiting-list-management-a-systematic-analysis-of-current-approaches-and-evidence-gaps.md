# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Analyze current patient waiting-list prioritization approaches, evidence quality and implementation gaps.
- healthcare setting: Elective procedure waiting-list management across published studies.
- patient population: Not a primary patient cohort; 21 studies on prioritization systems.
- sample size: 21 studies published from 2010 to 2025.
- study design: Systematic analysis/review.

# Data
- data source: PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science searches.
- variables used: Prioritization method, evidence type, implementation status, geographic setting, scoring/automated-system categories.
- outcomes measured: Dominant approaches, implementation rate and theory-practice gap.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Descriptive proportions across included studies.
- machine learning methods: Reviews ML/fuzzy logic and automated prioritization tools, but does not build a model.
- simulation methods: Not a simulation study.
- queueing methods: Waiting-list prioritization focus; not a formal queueing-theory paper.
- optimization methods: Reviews prioritization/decision tools, including automated systems.

# Results
- main findings: Most tools remain theoretical or point-based; implementation evidence is rare, especially in low-resource settings.
- quantitative outcomes: 71% of studies used point-based scoring; 5% reported actual implementation; 76% came from developed countries; 57% promoted automated systems.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Likely limited by included databases, English-language scope and conference-review depth.
- implicit limitations: Useful as a gap statement but the review itself may be less rigorous than journal systematic reviews.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 5
- justification: Directly supports the PhD argument that waiting-list tools often fail to become implementable decision support.
