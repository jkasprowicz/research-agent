# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Characterize the profile and demands of individuals on a municipal SUS speech-therapy waiting list and update queue status.
- healthcare setting: Medium-sized municipality in interior São Paulo, Brazil; speech-language pathology regulation/waiting list.
- patient population: Individuals referred by primary care and waiting for speech-language pathology care.
- sample size: 393 individuals initially; 147 removed from the list; 246 remained for analysis after update.
- study design: Descriptive study with telephone contact and waiting-list updating.

# Data
- data source: Municipal regulation/evaluation-control waiting list and telephone follow-up.
- variables used: Sex, age, initial complaint, current complaint, service area, queue status and scheduling status.
- outcomes measured: Profile of demand, discrepancies between referral and current complaint, exclusions from waiting list, scheduled appointments.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Descriptive statistics only.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No simulation component.
- queueing methods: Direct waiting-list update/cleanup; no formal queue model.
- optimization methods: Operational list-cleaning and demand characterization for service planning.

# Results
- main findings: Updating the list removed obsolete cases, identified demand profile and enabled scheduling of a substantial proportion of remaining users.
- quantitative outcomes: 147/393 removed; 71.9% of remaining users were children up to 11 years; 64.2% male; 26.8% had discrepancy between current complaint and referral; 65.9% were scheduled.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Single municipality; incomplete/outdated records; telephone-only contact may exclude unreachable users; descriptive analysis only.
- implicit limitations: Useful for operational realism but does not provide predictive or decision-support methodology.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 5
- justification: Direct SUS waiting-list evidence showing that queue data quality and list maintenance are central to any computational proposal.
