- year: 2026
- country: France

# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Develop and validate a data-driven queueing methodology bridging predictive/statistical modeling and queueing/simulation for ED crowding evaluation.
- healthcare setting: Major emergency department in eastern France.
- patient population: ED patients during a 28-week period.
- sample size: 34,358 ED arrivals from 14 Oct 2018 to 28 Apr 2019.
- study design: Data-driven queueing-network modeling and synchronous simulation case study.

# Data
- data source: Emergency department operational/patient-flow data.
- variables used: Arrival patterns, patient classes, stations/phases of care, doctor/nurse allocation, departure probabilities/rates and temporal indicators.
- outcomes measured: Crowding indicators, waiting times, occupancy and impact of unscheduled-care services.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Statistical modeling of queueing processes, departure rates and probabilities.
- machine learning methods: Contrasts with forecasting ML but does not primarily use black-box ML.
- simulation methods: Synchronous simulation algorithm applied to a queueing network.
- queueing methods: Data-driven queueing network model with patient classes and service stations.
- optimization methods: Designed for scenario evaluation and later decision-support/optimization applications.

# Results
- main findings: The model represented complex effects of arrivals and staff allocation and quantified the impact of new unscheduled-care services on ED crowding.
- quantitative outcomes: 34,358 patients; 5,627 class 1 and 28,731 class 0 reported in extracted results.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Needs validation in other systems and incorporation of more detailed variables while controlling model complexity.
- implicit limitations: ED-focused and technically demanding; direct transfer to SUS waiting lists requires longitudinal regulation-event data.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 4
- justification: Strong methodological bridge between prediction, queueing and simulation; not SUS-specific but directly useful for a hybrid decision-support framing.
