# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Review studies combining AI and simulation to optimize healthcare processes.
- healthcare setting: Healthcare process optimization across hospital departments and clinical pathways.
- patient population: Not a primary patient cohort; review of original studies.
- sample size: 2,506 records identified; 22 studies included.
- study design: Systematic review following PRISMA-style approach.

# Data
- data source: PubMed, IEEE Xplore and snowball search for 2014-2025 literature.
- variables used: AI method, simulation method, healthcare process, resource-allocation target, wait-time/patient-flow outcome.
- outcomes measured: Process efficiency, resource allocation, waiting time reduction and methodological trends.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Descriptive review synthesis and quality assessment.
- machine learning methods: Supervised learning was common among included studies.
- simulation methods: Discrete-event simulation and agent-based simulation were prominent.
- queueing methods: Queue behavior appears through process simulation/waiting-time outcomes; not necessarily analytical queueing.
- optimization methods: Healthcare process optimization and scenario analysis.

# Results
- main findings: AI + simulation is emerging and promising but still underused; studies concentrate on EDs and clinical pathways.
- quantitative outcomes: 22 studies included from 2,506 records; most published between 2021 and 2025.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Single-institution designs in many included studies and barriers related to interoperability, standardization and data integration.
- implicit limitations: Broad review; not specific to waiting lists or SUS regulation, so it should support methods rather than define the problem.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 4
- justification: Useful for justifying a hybrid ML/simulation decision-support program, but needs SUS-specific papers for domain grounding.
