- authors: Grace Lim; Annamarie J. Lim; Beth Quinn; Brendan Carvalho; Mark Zakowski; Grant C. Lynde
- year: 2023
- country: United States
- journal: BMC Health Services Research

# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Use queueing theory to identify optimal operating-room and staffing resources for a birth center.
- healthcare setting: Level 4 maternity center operating rooms.
- patient population: Labor and delivery OR activities and cesarean deliveries.
- sample size: 9,626 births/year context; 4,017 OR activities and 3,092 cesarean deliveries from July 2019 to June 2020.
- study design: Retrospective operational analysis using queueing theory.

# Data
- data source: Labor and delivery OR activity records.
- variables used: Arrival rate, service rate, number of OR teams/servers, safety thresholds and time windows.
- outcomes measured: Probability of waiting, staffing/space adequacy and safety-threshold feasibility.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Queueing-theory calculations of probabilities under different server-team assumptions.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No discrete-event simulation reported in the abstract.
- queueing methods: M/M/s-style/multichannel multiphase queueing analysis using arrival and service rates.
- optimization methods: Resource/staffing scenario assessment under predefined safety parameters.

# Results
- main findings: Queueing theory can support staffing decisions while accounting for daily peak demand and safety thresholds.
- quantitative outcomes: Arrival rate 0.45 patients/hour at peak and 0.34 over 24h; service rate 0.87 patients/hour; P0, P1 and P>=2 varied by peak versus full-day assumptions.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Only birth-center OR activity; excludes other anesthesiology responsibilities; queueing assumptions may not cover state-dependent arrivals.
- implicit limitations: Useful operational method, but specific to acute OR staffing rather than public waiting-list management.
