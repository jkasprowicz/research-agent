# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Identify factors associated with waiting time for scheduling oral biopsy after need identification in Brazilian public dental specialty centers.
- healthcare setting: Brazilian Dental Specialty Centers (CEO) in SUS.
- patient population: Dental specialty centers and users needing oral biopsy scheduling.
- sample size: National PMAQ-CEO data; exact number of CEO records should be verified in tables, but abstract reports center-level proportions.
- study design: Observational cross-sectional national-level analysis.

# Data
- data source: Secondary data from the 2nd cycle of PMAQ-CEO.
- variables used: Waiting time for oral biopsy scheduling, dentist workload, center coverage, primary-care oral health team reference, contextual variables.
- outcomes measured: Immediate versus non-immediate biopsy scheduling and associated center-level factors.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Simple logistic regression and multiple multilevel logistic regression.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No simulation component.
- queueing methods: No formal queueing model; direct analysis of scheduling delay.
- optimization methods: No optimization model; identifies service factors relevant to management.

# Results
- main findings: Non-immediate biopsy scheduling was common; municipal-only coverage was associated with longer wait for scheduling.
- quantitative outcomes: 51.8% of DSC had non-immediate scheduling; 58.1% of CEO had stomatology workload up to 20h/week; 67.1% had municipal-only coverage.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Secondary data and center-level variables may not capture all clinical/process details.
- implicit limitations: Good epidemiological/service analysis but not predictive, causal or workflow-aware decision support.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 4
- justification: Relevant SUS access-delay evidence, especially for modeling service characteristics and regulation context.
