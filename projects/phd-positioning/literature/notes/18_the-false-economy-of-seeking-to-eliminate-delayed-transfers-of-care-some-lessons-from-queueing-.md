- title: The False Economy of Seeking to Eliminate Delayed Transfers of Care: Some Lessons from Queueing Theory
- authors: Richard M. Wood; Alison L. Harper; Zehra Onen-Dumlu; Paul G. Forte; Martin Pitt; Christos Vasilakis
- year: 2022
- country: United Kingdom
- journal: Applied Health Economics and Health Policy
- source file: /Users/joaokasprowicz/Documents/Projects/research-agent/projects/phd-positioning/literature/pdfs/tier2/40258_2022_Article_777.pdf

# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Use queueing theory to show why eliminating delayed transfers of care may be economically inefficient.
- healthcare setting: Acute-to-community patient flow in England’s NHS.
- patient population: Patients ready for discharge from acute care requiring downstream community capacity.
- sample size: Model calibrated from multiple NHS/community data sources; no single primary cohort.
- study design: Queueing-theory economic modeling study.

# Data
- data source: Healthcare-system cost/capacity inputs from multiple sources.
- variables used: Acute discharge arrivals, community length of stay, downstream capacity, acute/community costs and tolerated queueing.
- outcomes measured: Economically optimal delayed-transfer level and cost savings.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Analytical queueing formulae and economic calculations.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No discrete-event simulation; analytical model.
- queueing methods: Central method: queueing theory for patient flow and capacity sizing.
- optimization methods: Economic capacity trade-off, showing optimal nonzero delay.

# Results
- main findings: Zero-delay targets can be economically irrational because variability requires excess downstream capacity that remains unused much of the time.
- quantitative outcomes: Model estimates annual NHS savings of £117m if DTOCs are reduced to the 12% of current levels considered economically essential.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Uses approximations and multiple calibration sources because no single source contained all data.
- implicit limitations: Not about waiting lists for elective access, but valuable for explaining why operational targets must account for stochastic variability.
