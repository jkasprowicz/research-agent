# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Identify factors associated with shorter waiting time for specialized oral healthcare services in Brazilian Dental Specialty Centers.
- healthcare setting: Dental Specialty Centers (CEO) in Brazilian SUS.
- patient population: Users of specialized oral healthcare services and center-level service characteristics.
- sample size: 1,097 Dental Specialty Centers evaluated in loco in 2018.
- study design: Descriptive and analytic exploratory secondary analysis.

# Data
- data source: 2nd phase of the PMAQ-CEO national evaluation.
- variables used: Specialty, CEO type/size, management experience, communication with primary care, route of access and service characteristics.
- outcomes measured: Waiting time for access to stomatology, surgery, endodontics, patients with special needs and periodontology.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Binary logistic regression by specialty.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No simulation component.
- queueing methods: No formal queueing model; empirical waiting-time/access analysis.
- optimization methods: No optimization model; identifies modifiable management/service factors.

# Results
- main findings: Waiting time varied by specialty and service organization; smaller centers and direct access routes were associated with shorter waits in some specialties.
- quantitative outcomes: Median waits: endodontics 30 days, stomatology 5 days. Type I CEO had shorter waits for patients with special needs, endodontics and surgery.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: No explicit limitation captured in extracted section; secondary analysis limitations likely include cross-sectional design and self-reported/center-level variables.
- implicit limitations: Associational evidence only; cannot directly optimize queues or predict individual waiting trajectories.
