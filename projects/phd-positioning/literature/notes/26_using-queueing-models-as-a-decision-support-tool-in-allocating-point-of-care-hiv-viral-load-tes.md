- title: Using queueing models as a decision support tool in allocating point-of-care HIV viral load testing machines in Kisumu County, Kenya
- authors: Yinsheng Wang et al.
- year: 2024
- country: Kenya / United States collaboration
- journal: Health Policy and Planning

# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Use queueing and optimization models to allocate point-of-care HIV viral-load testing machines under access, turnaround-time and cost constraints.
- healthcare setting: HIV viral-load testing network in Kisumu County, Kenya.
- patient population: HIV viral-load samples/patients served by centralized laboratories and POC hub facilities.
- sample size: Scenario/modeling study; exact sample volume should be checked in the article tables.
- study design: Decision-support modeling study informed by stakeholder discussions.

# Data
- data source: Stakeholder-informed model inputs and service-network data for HIV VL monitoring.
- variables used: Facility demand, batching/transport, testing-site waiting time, machine placement, costs and turnaround-time components.
- outcomes measured: Coverage/access, turnaround time and cost under alternative POC placement scenarios.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Scenario comparison and queueing/optimization outputs.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No primary DES in extracted abstract; model-based scenario analysis.
- queueing methods: Queueing theory incorporated into an optimization model.
- optimization methods: Integer programming for POC machine allocation.

# Results
- main findings: Turnaround time was driven mainly by batching/transport waiting and testing-site waiting; optimized allocation can support decision makers under resource constraints.
- quantitative outcomes: TAT components reported: facility batching/transport waiting 78.7%-91.8% across scenarios; testing-site waiting 7.5%-18.7%; transportation 0.7%-2.6% depending on scenario.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Model depends on input assumptions and stakeholder-informed parameters.
- implicit limitations: Laboratory network, not healthcare waiting lists, but very strong exemplar of queueing + optimization + decision support in constrained public health systems.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 4
- justification: Excellent transferable model for decision-support architecture and resource-allocation logic in public systems.
