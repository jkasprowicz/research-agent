- authors: Rosa Ladi Lisboa; Alexandra Jochims Kruel; Júlia Barreto Marciniak; Adriana Aparecida Paz
- year: 2022
- country: Brazil

# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Describe actions of a regulatory nurse in managing elective-surgery waiting lists in a public hospital.
- healthcare setting: Large public general hospital in Southern Brazil.
- patient population: Elective-surgery waiting-list users and hospital teams involved in regulation; experience report rather than patient cohort.
- sample size: The setting reportedly had about 2,000 patients on lists, with many waiting >180 days and 40% waiting >2 years.
- study design: Experience report.

# Data
- data source: Description of regulatory nurse actions and local management experience.
- variables used: Waiting-list governance, discrepancies, access equity, safety, feedback with medical teams, autonomy and communication.
- outcomes measured: Reported reduction in waits, elimination of access discrepancies and improved safety/communication.

# Methods
- statistical methods: No statistical analysis; authors note data were not tested for significance.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: No simulation component.
- queueing methods: No formal queue model.
- optimization methods: Process-management intervention through regulatory role and continuous list management.

# Results
- main findings: Continuous systematic management can reduce wait times, correct inequities and improve coordination, but evidence is descriptive.
- quantitative outcomes: Approximate local context: 2,000 patients on lists; 40% waiting more than two years. No inferential outcome metrics.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Only most engaged specialties reported; no statistical test of whether changes were significant.
- implicit limitations: Low evidentiary strength for algorithmic decisions but high value for understanding workflow and governance roles.

# Relevance for SUS Waiting Lists
- relevance score (1-5): 4
- justification: Important qualitative/workflow evidence for SUS waiting-list management, especially for avoiding a purely algorithmic proposal.
