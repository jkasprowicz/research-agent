# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Evaluate whether queueing theory can compare interventions to optimize hospital medication reconciliation and reduce incomplete discharge reconciliation.
- healthcare setting: Hospital pharmacy/medication-reconciliation workflow.
- patient population: Hospital patients requiring medication reconciliation at admission/discharge.
- sample size: Computer experiment based on hospital information-system data; exact patient count should be checked in tables.
- study design: Queueing-theory computer experiment comparing workflow interventions.

# Data
- data source: Hospital information-system data and measured medication-reconciliation times.
- variables used: Age groups, length of stay, arrival/service rates, pharmacy technician allocation, conversation mode and working schedule.
- outcomes measured: Percentage of patients discharged before MedRec completion.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Queueing model scenario analysis.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: Computer experiment; not described as DES in extracted abstract.
- queueing methods: Central method: queueing theory for workflow intervention evaluation.
- optimization methods: Comparison of staffing/schedule/workflow interventions.

# Results
- main findings: Queueing theory allowed non-disruptive comparison of interventions; reallocating/scheduling changes could substantially reduce incomplete MedRec.
- quantitative outcomes: Original incomplete discharge MedRec about 37.2%; intervention scenarios reduced it to approximately 16% in the best case extracted.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Assumptions about telephone conversations and pandemic-induced workflow changes; patient acceptability and practical constraints noted.
- implicit limitations: Workflow-specific but valuable for non-invasive evaluation of process interventions before implementation.
