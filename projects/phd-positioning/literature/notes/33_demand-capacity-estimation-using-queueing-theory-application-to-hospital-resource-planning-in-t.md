# Study Characteristics
- study objective: Develop and validate a mathematical model to estimate emergency physician demand after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes.
- healthcare setting: Hatay Mustafa Kemal University Health Practice and Research Hospital after earthquake disaster.
- patient population: Estimated injured/emergency patients after earthquake exposure.
- sample size: Model uses exposed population/casualty estimates and hospital service assumptions rather than a conventional enrolled patient sample.
- study design: Mathematical demand-capacity modeling study.

# Data
- data source: USGS PAGER exposure data, TurkStat household/building data, empirical fragility functions, casualty ratios and hospital physician-service assumptions.
- variables used: Exposure intensity, building vulnerability, collapse probabilities, fatality/injury ratios, arrival rate and physician service rate.
- outcomes measured: Required number of emergency physicians under post-disaster demand scenarios.

# Methods
- statistical methods: Mathematical modeling and validation against actual data where available.
- machine learning methods: No machine-learning component.
- simulation methods: Scenario/modeling approach; no ML simulation component.
- queueing methods: M/M/s queueing model for physician demand estimation.
- optimization methods: Resource planning/demand-capacity estimation for staffing decisions.

# Results
- main findings: Queueing-based demand-capacity modeling can support physician workforce planning and disaster resilience.
- quantitative outcomes: Extracted text reports model structure; detailed physician-number outputs should be checked in results tables.

# Limitations
- stated limitations: Fragility functions may not fully represent regional building characteristics; HAZUS/data availability limitations.
- implicit limitations: Disaster emergency planning is distant from elective SUS waiting lists, but methodology supports demand-capacity reasoning.
